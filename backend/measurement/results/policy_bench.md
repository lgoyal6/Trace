# Retrieval policy A/B - breadth vs depth at a fixed token budget

Generated by `python -m backend.measurement.run_policy_bench`. Raw numbers:
[`policy_bench.json`](policy_bench.json).

## The claim

Rare conditions are the ones a top-10 cut is structurally hostile to. The
corpus makes the reason plain - Neurolymphomatosis has **4** papers and Primary
angiitis of CNS has **5**, against **40** each for the four common conditions,
in a 329-paper corpus. README.md's founding citation is a published pilot that
failed on scalp angiosarcoma *because the condition was rare*; our own gold set
reproduces that class of failure.

Looking at more papers fixes it, and normally costs more. It does not have to.
`backend/app/retrieval/policy.py` moves two dials in opposite directions:

| policy | `top_k` | `compress_top_n` | meaning |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `TIGHT` | 10 | 4 | today's retrieval breadth, 4 sentences per abstract |
| `GENEROUS` | 30 | 1 | 3× the papers, 1 sentence each |

## Result (28-query gold set, n=20 rare)

| | TIGHT | GENEROUS | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| prompt tokens | 42,401 | 42,110 | **−0.69%** |
| summary cost (USD) | 0.303844 | 0.302796 | **−0.34%** |
| mean rare-condition recall | 0.4118 | 0.7475 | **+81.52%** |
| queries surfacing zero relevant papers | 3 | 1 | **−2** |
| rare recall per dollar | 1.36 | 2.47 | **+82.15%** |

**Rare-condition recall goes up 81.5% and the bill goes down 0.34%.** Recall
improved on 17 of the 20 rare-condition queries, held steady on 2 already at
ceiling, and regressed on none.

### Iso-cost is an aggregate, not a per-query guarantee

Worth stating before someone runs one query and catches it: the −0.69% holds
**across the 28-query set**, not on every query. Per query the generous/tight
token ratio ranges **0.795× to 1.382×**, median **0.992×**, and generous is the
more expensive arm on **14 of 28**.

The cause is `compress_abstract`'s refusal to trim abstracts with fewer than
three sentences (`_MIN_SENTENCES_TO_COMPRESS`) - those pass through whole
regardless of `compress_top_n`, and a 30-paper set simply contains more of them
than a 10-paper one. So the sentence budget is an upper bound that short
abstracts escape.

The defensible claim is therefore *"3× the papers for the same token budget on
average, and a large rare-recall gain"* - not *"always cheaper"*.

Queries `GENEROUS` rescued from surfacing *no* relevant paper at all:

- `Alzheimer's disease FDG-PET temporal parietal pattern`
- `amyloid PET typical Alzheimer's disease biomarker pattern`

## `GENEROUS` was measured, not chosen

k=30/n=1 is where the sweep lands, not a number picked to look good:

| policy | `top_k` | `top_n` | rare recall | zero-hit | tokens | cost (USD) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| tight | 10 | 4 | 0.4118 | 3 | 42,401 | 0.303844 |
| k20n2 | 20 | 2 | 0.6431 | 2 | 49,215 | 0.328374 |
| **generous** | **30** | **1** | **0.7475** | **1** | **42,110** | **0.302796** |
| k40n1 | 40 | 1 | 0.8079 | 1 | 55,586 | 0.351310 |
| k40n2 | 40 | 2 | 0.8079 | 2 | 95,227 | 0.494017 |

Breadth keeps buying recall past k=30 - it just stops being free. k=40/n=1 buys
a further +0.06 rare recall for **+31% tokens**. `GENEROUS` is the widest
setting still at or under `TIGHT`'s budget, which is the only reason the recall
gain can be called free.

## Running the two arms live

`POST /query` (and `/query/stream`) takes an optional `policy` label. Omit it
for today's behaviour; pass `"tight"` or `"generous"` to run an arm and get back
what it did. Unknown labels are a 422, never a silent fallback.

```jsonc
// POST /query  { "query": "...", "session_id": "s", "user_id": "u",
//                "policy": "generous" }
"policy": {
  "label": "generous", "topK": 30, "compressTopN": 1, "papersInPrompt": 30,
  "promptTokensBeforeCompression": 7226, "promptTokensAfterCompression": 1578,
  "tokensSaved": 5648, "reductionPct": 78.16
}
```

Same query on the other arm - `"policy": "tight"` - gives 10 papers in **1,680**
tokens. So on a single live request: **3× the papers, 6% fewer tokens.** That is
the gold-set result reproducing per-request, and it is the 30-second demo.

Contract note: both the request and response fields are additive and optional,
logged in `Decisions.md` per `plan-v2/00-SHARED-CONTRACTS.md` §4. Nothing changes
for a caller that doesn't pass `policy`.

## On the recall denominator

`run_gate.py` reports recall@10 with `denom = min(10, total_relevant)`. That
convention cannot be compared across arms - it would hand `TIGHT` a denominator
of 10 and `GENEROUS` one of 30 for the same condition. This bench uses
`recall_true` (**denominator = all relevant papers in the corpus**, identical
for both arms) as the cross-arm number, and carries `recall_capped`
(run_gate's convention) purely so the `TIGHT` arm can be checked against the
already-published 0.60 / 0.67 figures.

`zero_hit_queries` needs no denominator argument at all: it counts queries where
the arm surfaced not one relevant paper. That is the published failure mode, and
it is binary.

## What this does NOT show

- **Answer quality.** No LLM is called in this bench. It measures what reaches
  the summary prompt, not what the model writes from it. Whether higher
  rare-condition recall in the prompt yields a better *sourced summary* needs a
  live run scored by `citation_check`, which needs Snowflake credentials.
- **Today's exact summary prompt.** `pipeline.run_query` cuts to
  `SUMMARY_TOP_N = 5` full abstracts and applies no compression - `compress.py`
  was built, tested and measured but never wired into prompt assembly until
  now. `TIGHT` is the policy-space representation of today's *retrieval*, not a
  byte-for-byte replay of a live summary call.
- **Live retrieval.** Scoring is `backend.contracts.fakes.FakeRetrieval`
  (deterministic token overlap + the real rarity multiplier), the same
  zero-credential stand-in `run_gate.py` uses. `CortexSearchRetriever` applies
  the same `top_k`, so the policy transfers, but the recall numbers would need
  re-running against the live search service to be quoted as production figures.
- **One query stays broken.** `neurolymphomatosis leptomeningeal PET` surfaces
  zero relevant papers under **every** policy in the sweep, up to k=40. Its 4
  corpus papers do not lexically overlap the query wording under
  `FakeRetrieval`. That is a scorer failure, not a breadth failure, and no
  amount of `top_k` fixes it - worth re-checking against live Cortex Search,
  whose embeddings may catch what token overlap cannot.

## Cost is priced, not guessed

Every dollar figure is the real `compute_cost_usd()` against the published
`claude-3-5-sonnet` `MODEL_PRICING` row (1.8 credits/Mtok in, 9.0 out, $2/credit),
with completion tokens held constant at 300 across both arms - policy changes
the prompt, not the answer length. The same unreconciled-list-price caveat in
`decision.md` §5 applies: published rates, not this account's billed rates.
